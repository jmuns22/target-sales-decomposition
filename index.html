Target Corporation — FY2026 Analysis

Is Target's sales recovery real, or a favorable quarter dressed up by a tax refund?

Target reported comp sales up 3.8% and EPS up 100% this quarter. This project checks both numbers against public filings, a competitor comparison, and the company's own trend — built entirely from data Target has already made public.

1. Target closed a competitive gap against Walmart

Comparable sales, prior quarter to current quarter, both companies

So what A year ago Target was losing ground to Walmart; today it's ahead. Since both companies faced the same tariffs and the same economy, this points to something Target-specific working — not just a favorable market. Worth watching whether this holds for another two quarters before treating it as a durable trend.

2. Most of the profit jump was a refund, not the business improving

Reported EPS growth, decomposed

So what Investors and press repeated the "profit doubled" headline. The real, repeatable improvement was closer to 20% — still strong, but leadership and analysts should anchor guidance on that number, not the refund-inflated one.

3. The growth is coming from more shoppers, not bigger baskets

Comp sales split into traffic vs. average ticket, four representative quarters

So what This quarter's growth is almost entirely more visits, not more spend per visit. That suggests an opportunity: basket-building tactics — bundling, cross-merchandising, targeted promotions at checkout — could convert existing traffic gains into ticket gains too, compounding the recovery rather than relying on visit growth alone.
Target's own engineering team has published on treating fulfillment allocation as an active optimization problem — a hybrid algorithm recovering ~91% of the cost gap between brute-force and greedy methods at ~8% of the compute cost (Target Tech, Apr 2026). Rising traffic without matching fulfillment efficiency is exactly the kind of pressure that work is built to absorb.

Is the competitive gap statistically real? (Panel regression)

A diff-in-difference regression on 26 real quarterly observations (2022–2026, both companies) tests whether Target's change relative to Walmart, after the boycott began, is distinguishable from normal quarter-to-quarter variation.

Honest read: Target has significantly and durably trailed Walmart across this multi-year window — that part is statistically solid. Whether the recent narrowing is a real, lasting shift or normal noise cannot yet be confirmed with the data currently public. The point estimate is positive and consistent with the story; the confidence interval is too wide to call it proven.

Limitation: sourced entirely from public quarterly filings; several individual quarters for both companies remain unavailable and are excluded rather than estimated.
Does the recovery beat Target's own historical trend? (Forecast)

A trend model (Holt's linear method, no seasonal component — 12 pre-boycott quarters isn't enough data to reliably estimate seasonality) was trained on Target's comp sales before the boycott and used to forecast what the following quarters "should" have looked like if nothing unusual happened.

Honest read: Three consecutive quarters underperformed Target's own trend during the boycott window, then the most recent quarter beat it. The pattern is directionally consistent with a real drag followed by a real recovery, but every actual value falls inside the model's 95% band — none of these gaps clear statistical significance individually. Reported as a supporting pattern, not proof.

Limitations

  • All figures sourced from public filings only — Target's internal store-level, SKU-level, and customer-level data would materially sharpen this analysis and isn't available here.
  • The competitive-gap regression (n=26) cannot statistically confirm the recent narrowing is a lasting trend rather than noise; the confidence interval is wide.
  • The forecast model uses no seasonal component, since 12 pre-boycott quarters can't reliably support one — treat its uncertainty band as a lower bound, not a tight estimate.
  • Several individual quarters (both companies) remain unlogged due to public sourcing gaps; these are stated openly rather than filled with estimates.
  • The A/B testing module uses public marketing data, not a Target-run experiment — it demonstrates method, not access to Target's actual test results.
  • The boycott's demand impact is inferred through a competitor comparison, not directly measured — no source independently quantifies its dollar impact on Target's revenue.
